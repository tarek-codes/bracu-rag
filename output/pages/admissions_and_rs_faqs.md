## 4. Academic Eligibility & Minimum Requirements

### Q8. What are the requirements to apply for EEE/ECE/CSE/Physics/APE/CS/Mathematics in Undergraduate level?

1. **EEE, ECE, CSE, APE, and Physics:**
   - Candidates must have a **minimum 'B' grade in Physics & Mathematics** in HSC / Equivalent.
   - Candidates from 'A'-Levels must have a **minimum 'C' grade in Physics & Mathematics**.
   - *Special Remedial Requirement for EEE and ECE:* Candidates applying for BSc in Electronic & Communication Engineering (ECE) and Electrical & Electronic Engineering (EEE) who have Physics and Mathematics but **do not have Chemistry** at HSC or 'A'-Levels must take a compulsory remedial course in Chemistry in addition to the standard courses required for the program.
2. **Computer Science (CS) and Mathematics:**
   - Candidates must have a **minimum 'B' grade in Mathematics** at HSC / Equivalent.
   - Candidates from 'A'-Levels must have a **minimum 'C' grade in Mathematics**.

---

### Q9. What are the requirements to apply for Biotechnology/Microbiology in Undergraduate level?

- Candidates for **BSc in Biotechnology** and **BSc in Microbiology** must have a **minimum 'C' grade in Biology and Chemistry** at HSC / 'A'-Levels / Equivalent.
- Candidates who did **not** take Mathematics at HSC or 'A'-Levels must take a compulsory remedial course in Mathematics in addition to standard degree requirements.

---

### Q10. What are the requirements to apply for Pharmacy in Undergraduate level?

Candidates for **Bachelor of Pharmacy (Hons)** must satisfy all of the following requirements:
1. **Aggregate GPA:** Minimum aggregate GPA of **8.00 out of 10.00** (on a 10.0 scale combining SSC/O-Level and HSC/A-Level).
2. **Individual Exam GPA:** No individual GPA lower than **3.50** in SSC/O-Level/Equivalent and HSC/A-Level/Equivalent.
3. **Chemistry & Biology Combined:** Minimum aggregate GPA of **9.00 out of 10.00** in Chemistry and Biology combined.
4. **Mathematics Requirement:** Minimum GPA of **3.50 in Mathematics** in both SSC and HSC (on a 5.0 scale).
5. **'O' and 'A' Level Requirements:**
   - Minimum **'B' grade** in Chemistry and Biology.
   - Minimum **'C' grade** in Mathematics.
6. **Remedial Course:** Candidates not having Mathematics at HSC or 'A'-Levels will be required to take an extra 3-credit remedial course in Mathematics related to pharmaceutical sciences.

---

### Q11. How can I calculate my GPA of ‘O’/‘A’ levels for the admission application in BRAC University?

- **Minimum GPA Requirement:** Undergraduate programs require a **minimum GPA of 2.50 in ‘O’ Level and ‘A’ Level separately**.
- **Counted Subjects:**
  - **‘O’ Level:** Best **5 subjects** are counted.
  - **‘A’ Level:** Best **2 subjects** are counted.
- **Subject-Wise Rules:** Applicants must fulfill all subject-specific prerequisite requirements (e.g. Physics, Mathematics, Biology, Chemistry). **Subjects with an 'E' Grade are not considered.**

#### BRAC University Grade Point Scale:
| Letter Grade | Grade Points |
| :---: | :---: |
| **A** | **5** |
| **B** | **4** |
| **C** | **3** |
| **D** | **2** |
| **E** | *Not Considered (0)* |

#### Calculation Example (Best 5 'O' Level Subjects):

| Subject | Letter Grade | Points |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Subject 1 | A | 5 |
| Subject 2 | B | 4 |
| Subject 3 | C | 3 |
| Subject 4 | D | 2 |
| Subject 5 | C | 3 |
| **Total Scale Points** | — | **17** |
| **Calculated GPA** | — | **17 / 5 = 3.40** |

## 6. English Proficiency & Pre-University Program

### Q26. Is there any provisional admission for those who do not have the required level of proficiency in English language?

Many students wishing to join the university might not have the required standard of proficiency in the English language. If applicants fail to attain the minimum standard of English proficiency required by the university during the admission evaluation, they may be offered provisional admission conditional upon attending **remedial English courses** prior to standard university coursework. At the conclusion of this course, students must take an English proficiency evaluation; upon qualifying, they are admitted into regular BRAC University degree study.

---

### Q27. What is Pre-University Course for Undergraduate Programs?

A **Pre-University course** is designed for candidates who proved themselves academically competent in their respective disciplines during the admission test but did not meet the required threshold in English.
- **Format & Duration:** A **12-week intensive program** conducted by the BRAC Institute of Languages (BIL).
- **Core Purpose:** Assists students to become active, independent learners, critical thinkers, responsible and thoughtful adults, and successful university scholars.
- **Student Demographics:** Particularly transformative for students coming from mainstream Bangla-medium academic backgrounds to thrive in an English-medium university environment.

# Part 2: Residential Semester (RS) FAQs

## 1. Timing, Eligibility & Mandatory Policy

### RS Q1. When do students go to Savar for the Residential Semester?

Students are eligible to attend the Residential Semester (RS) in their **third semester**, after completing their first and second semesters at the main Mohakhali/Dhaka campus to a satisfactory standard.

#### Eligibility Criteria:
- Attained a minimum Cumulative Grade Point Average (**CGPA**) of **1.50**.
- Successfully **passed the ENG091 course**.

---

### RS Q2. Is the Residential Semester compulsory?

**Yes. The Residential Semester is 100% compulsory** for all registered undergraduate students of BRAC University. Satisfactory completion of the residential term is an essential degree requirement.

---

## 2. Savar Campus Location & Transportation

### RS Q3. Where is the Savar Campus located?

- **Location:** Situated away from urban congestion, BRAC University’s Savar Campus is located in the rural village of **Khagan on the Ashulia-Birulia Road in Savar**, approximately one hour’s drive from Dhaka.
- **Arrival Transportation:** On the first day of the semester, **students must use BRAC University’s designated transport service** to join and reach the Savar Campus.

---

## 3. Academic Structure, Courses & Mandatory Attendance

### RS Q4. How many academic courses must students take during the Residential Semester?

The core compulsory courses at the Residential Campus include:
1. **Bangla (BNG103)**
2. **English (ENG102)**
3. **Bangladesh Studies (EMB101)**
4. **Ethics & Culture (HUM103)**

#### Updated Curriculum Option (Effective Fall 2024):
- Starting from **Fall 2024**, students who are officially exempt from taking Bangla or English may be offered an alternative course: **Civic Engagement and Action (BU201)**.
- BU201 counts towards **General Education (GenEd) credits under Stream 4**. For students taking this course, it is treated as an elective from Stream 4.

#### Compulsory Field Visits:
- In addition to classroom lectures, there are **compulsory study tours and field visits to rural areas** that give students hands-on understanding of rural life, national development processes, and the cultural heritage of Bangladesh.

#### Course Materials:
- Students must purchase course materials from the **Ayesha Abed Library at Savar Campus** (materials are available several days prior to class commencement).

---

### RS Q5. What percentage of attendance is needed in order to pass?

A student **must attend at least 90 percent of classes** in order to be allowed to sit for final examinations. Failure to meet the 90% attendance standard results in examination disbarment.

---

## 4. Experiential Learning Activities (ELA) & Awards

### RS Q6. What are the Experiential Learning Activities (ELA) and their purposes?

The RS curriculum gives **equal importance** to academic coursework and **Experiential Learning Activities (ELA)** that develop students’ social, interpersonal, and leadership skills.

#### Core Activities Include:
- On-campus and off-campus social learning labs
- Study tours and institutional / organizational site visits
- Structured co-curricular activities
- Village immersion programs

> **Mandatory Rule:** Participation in **all co-curricular activities is mandatory** for all students.

---

### RS Q7. Why are activities outside of the campus important?

Activities outside of the campus are essential for students to acquire firsthand experiential knowledge of their subject matter beyond traditional classrooms (e.g., students participate in full day trips and immersion in neighboring rural villages).

---

### RS Q8. How is participation in activities rewarded?

- **Vice Chancellor’s Certificates:** Students who demonstrate exceptional performance across academic, leadership, and community endeavors are awarded prestigious Vice Chancellor's certificates.
- **Activity Completion Certificates & Prizes:** Awarded to students who successfully complete specialized residential modules (e.g., yoga, dance, martial arts, cultural arts).
- **Disqualification Rule:** Students who miss **two or more activity classes** will not receive the completion certificate.

---

## 5. Dormitory Regulations, Leave & Visitors

### RS Q9. What are the dormitory opening and closing times?

- **Dormitory Gate Opening:** **6:30 AM** in the morning
- **Dormitory Gate Closing (Strict Curfew):** **10:00 PM sharp**
- Students are personally responsible for reaching their respective dormitories before 10:00 PM.

---

### RS Q10. How frequently can students leave Savar Campus to visit home?

The RS semester is designed to be **entirely residential**, and students are **strictly discouraged from leaving campus**.
- **Leave of Absence:** Students are granted short leave only in genuine emergencies and exceptional circumstances, subject to formal written leave application approval by the campus authority.
- **Overstay Penalties:** Any overstay of approved leave without valid justification is considered a serious breach of discipline, and offending students will not be granted further leave for the remainder of the semester.

---

### RS Q11. When and how frequently can students receive outside guests?

Only **nominated visitors (strictly parents or officially designated legal guardians)** are permitted to visit students, exclusively on scheduled **Parents’ Days**:
- **Designated Visiting Hours:** Typically Fridays and Saturdays from **3:00 PM to 5:00 PM** (or 3:30 PM to 5:00 PM on specified subsequent Fridays).
- *Orientation Reference Schedule:* E.g., Induction weekends (May 11 & 12 from 3:00 PM–5:00 PM) and follow-up Fridays (June 01, July 06, and July 27 from 3:30 PM–5:00 PM). Specific dates are announced in the official semester packet.

---

## 6. Packing Checklist, Prohibited Items & Student Conduct

### RS Q12. What personal items should students bring with them?

#### Recommended Items to Bring:
- **Clothing:** Formal wear, everyday casual clothes, and sportswear / athletic clothing.
- **Door Security:** **Two sets of medium or small padlocks and keys** (for securing dormitory room doors and personal lockers).
- **Photographs & Health Card:** **5 passport-sized photographs** and a **photocopy of their completed Health Card**.
- **Registration Documents:** Bank payment/deposit slip and official grade sheet (to be submitted to respective dorm tutors during dormitory registration).
- **Personal Care & Study:** Personal toiletries, writing stationery for class, and necessary personal prescription medicines.
- **Room Furnishings Provided:** Two- or three-seated rooms equipped with beds, bedcovers, pillows, pillow covers, and mosquito nets are provided by the campus.
- **Valuables Warning:** Students are discouraged from bringing expensive cell phones, cameras, or valuable jewellery. Management accepts no responsibility for lost or misplaced personal items. Students must bring lightweight luggage that can be easily carried to the dorms.

#### Strictly Prohibited Items:
The following electrical and recreational items are forbidden inside dormitories:
- Desktop PCs
- Sound systems / loud speakers
- Electric room heaters
- Electric clothing irons
- Playing cards / gambling materials
- Illegal drugs or narcotics

#### Student Code of Conduct:
Students must rigorously refrain from any of the following disciplinary infractions:
- Plagiarism and academic cheating
- Eve teasing, sexual harassment, or bullying
- Unfair means in examinations
- Disrespect or misbehavior toward teachers, house tutors, or university staff
- Vandalism or damaging campus assets
- Theft or unauthorized possession of others' property
- Physical violence, fighting, or assault
- Political activism or political organizing on campus
- Fabrication of visitor profiles or unauthorized visitors
- Socially unacceptable relations with other students
- Entering dormitories of the opposite gender
- Leaving campus grounds without formal written authorization
- Racism, discrimination, slurs, or foul language
- Gambling and viewing adult/explicit movies
- Smoking in non-designated / smoke-free areas
- Writing or defacing campus walls
- Possessing or bringing any kind of weapons or firearms onto campus

> Strict disciplinary action will be taken against any student transgressing these campus rules.

#### Strict Zero-Tolerance Drug Policy:
- Bringing, consuming, possessing, or sharing illicit drugs or alcohol on Savar Campus is **strictly prohibited**.
- Any student caught violating this policy will be brought before the University Disciplinary Committee and **may be expelled from BRAC University**.
- Suspected substance abusers may be required at any time to undergo mandatory **urine or blood toxicology testing**. Positive test results will lead to rigorous disciplinary proceedings and expulsion.
- Only prescription medications formally prescribed by a qualified medical practitioner and registered on the Health Card are exempt.

---

### RS Q13. What is the protocol for students facing problems with fellow students or the dorm?

BRAC University has a multi-tiered escalation hierarchy for student welfare:
1. **First Line of Redress:** Report issues to **House Tutors**, resident **Faculty Members**, or **Psychosocial Counselors**.
2. **Second Line of Redress:** If the problem remains unresolved, escalate the matter to the **Assistant Campus Superintendent (ACS)**.
3. **Executive Escalation:** In extreme, severe, or unresolved cases, report directly to the **Campus Superintendent (CS)**.

#### Residential, Dining & Gathering Spaces:
- Comfortable, separated residential dormitories for male and female students and faculty
- **Four large centralized dining halls** providing hygienic meals
- Large auditorium for guest symposiums, presentations, and cultural programs

#### Medical Services:
- Dedicated campus medical center with a full-time **resident doctor** and **staff nurse**.
- Institutional tie-up and emergency transfer protocols with **Enam Medical College and Hospital** in Savar.
- *Medical Expenses:* Students are responsible for their own individual hospital bills and specialist medications.
- *Health Card Protocol:* Parents are encouraged to disclose all health concerns (including past illnesses or drug concerns) on the Health Card. Any scheduled appointments with outside doctors must be declared on the Health Card at registration; otherwise, medical leave during the semester will not be granted.

#### Psychosocial Support Services:
- Savar Campus has **two full-time psychosocial counselors (one male and one female)** to support students experiencing emotional, social, or academic challenges.
- Professional counseling provides students with clarity, coping strategies, conflict management skills, and emotional support.

#### Sports & Athletic Facilities:
- Dedicated outdoor playing fields and courts for football, cricket, handball, volleyball, and badminton.
- Indoor facilities for table tennis and carom.
- *Sports Gear Requirement:* While courts and pitches are provided, students must bring their own sports equipment (e.g. balls, bats, racquets, shuttlecocks).
